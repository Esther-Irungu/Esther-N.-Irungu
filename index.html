How data can bring policy closer to people

My training in economics and statistics taught me to look for the systems and incentives behind outcomes. Applied policy work raised a harder question: why can a policy look sound on paper and still miss what people experience? That question drew me toward social data science—using data to test assumptions while staying close to the realities the numbers represent.

At KIPPRA, I examined why economic growth was not translating into better-quality jobs for young people. The analysis brought together labour surveys, enterprise censuses and education records, using growth accounting and shift-share analysis to look across industries and firm sizes. We found job creation concentrated in low-productivity sectors while productivity gains clustered in capital-intensive industries creating fewer opportunities. Presenting the findings to Employment and Skills Task Forces helped move discussion beyond job counts toward productivity-linked employment strategies, including SME technology adoption and incentives for formalization.

My own path also runs from mentee to mentor. A 12-session Global Mentorship Initiative programme with a mentor from Microsoft UK helped me connect technical ability with confidence and professional presence. I carry that lesson into my mentoring through GMI and HER Lab, supporting others to build both capability and confidence.

That is the thread through my work today: ask who benefits and who is left out, bring the right evidence and local knowledge into the same conversation, then help turn the answer into policy and programmes people can use.
